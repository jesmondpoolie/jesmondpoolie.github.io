While technical accuracy and diagnostic yield in non-invasive imaging are extensively researched, the patient’s lived experience navigating these diagnostic environments remains fragmented across disparate clinical literature.

To address this, we registered our review protocol on the [Open Science Framework (OSF)](https://osf.io/qpj78/overview):  
**Adult Patient Experience in Non-Invasive Diagnostic Imaging – A scoping review protocol**.

---

### Why a Scoping Review?

A scoping review is the ideal evidence synthesis method here because our objective is to map the volume, nature, and characteristics of existing literature across diverse study designs, rather than evaluate a single clinical outcome.

We follow the **JBI (Joanna Briggs Institute)** scoping review methodology and report in line with the **PRISMA-ScR** framework.

---

### Key Review Parameters (PCC Framework)

- **Population:** Adult patients (aged 18+) undergoing diagnostic imaging.
- **Concept:** Lived patient experience—capturing communication, information provision, physical comfort, sensory environment, and procedural anxiety.
- **Context:** Any clinical setting delivering non-invasive diagnostic imaging modalities, including:
  - Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
  - Computed Tomography (CT)
  - Diagnostic Ultrasound / Echocardiography
  - Nuclear Medicine / PET / SPECT

---

### Current Progress

Data extraction is underway using **Covidence**, categorising findings to identify common experience touchpoints before, during, and after imaging procedures.
